### Pointer terminology and `dangling`

Hristian: The current situation is, we have 2 issues. One is for removing the indirection mumbo-jumbo from years ago. The other one is fixing inaccurate in a recent set of rules.

I've noticed a couple of things in the Reference. It intentionally keeps function pointer types separate from pointer types. I think fn pointers are called pointers by coincidence, and they don't share the semantics with raw pointers and references. And I think we should do the same.

Pete: Totally agree; this mirrors my observation.

Hristian: That takes care of 715. I'll make the needed changes. Preserve raw pointers, references, function pointers distinctions.

Hristian: For 716, we're missing UB in the FLS if we want to properly explain what Ralf did in the Zulip thread. Since we've touched UB, there have been 2 new section in the Reference dealing with pointers. The next step is to bring that stuff in and resolve the "user issue". We also mixed some things up in the FLS about pointers/references. The notions of pointer/references should be in the values chapter. Also thinking about adding a pointers section in the values chapter. We can put the UB stuff there.

Hristian: Thinking of doing one big change instead of two smaller ones.

Tshepang: How is this structured in the Reference? It could be good to follow that if the structure's good.

Hristian: There's a dedicated part for all UBs across all of the Reference. There are separate sections for pointer types and function pointer types. Not sure if they have something for references.

Pete: It says "all pointers are explicit 1st class values", and there's probably a further description of the different types of pointers.

Pete: So we might want one big change because it might affect many scattered things.

Hristian: Sort out pointers vs pointer types first. Batch number two could be the UB.

Pete: Makes sense as long as you think it's feasible. (Which you do.)

### Alignment

Thepang: We should talk to TC about alignment. Do we want to be closer to the Reference or the Reference to be close to us?

Pete: From a discussion with t-lang-docs, I got a vibe that they're open to us harmonizing, but it's likely that we have to create a mapping to understand the parity. And then we can figure out the amount of work to bring them to parity, and whether that will be a hard switch or some other way.

Hristian: Sounds like you're talking about traceability, something close to certification. Establishing that would mean that someone reads the reference...

Hristian: Use the hard links from the Reference and institute them into the FLS as a first step to find mapping / parity.

Hristian: It's going to take a lot of time given that we don't mirror the structure of the Reference. 2 outcomes: 1) We have to plug in all the holes we have; 2) Tone down the things we overdo. After that, do we erge both documents? One should become the official reference. I think the FLS shouldn't be that becasue it's super dry. For a new Rust programmer, it's going to be a nightmare to navigate. But it's good for certification. I don't know if the ultimate outcome is worth the effort.

Pete: I agree. The FLS is dry and isn't friendly to someone who'd probably want to use the Reference. The problem is, you can't get input from certifying bodies before you show them the result of the work... Would an assessor accept something like the Reference? And would the community accept something like the FLS?

Hristian: I can ask someone from the certification team.

Pete: Some of the precursor work is valuable to understand the parity, etc. But how far should we take it?

Tshepang: I've heard from ?? that any requirements doc would do. It doesn't have to follow a rigid format, as long as there's traceability.
