Hristian: There's a different question we haven't resolved. TC, I [posted the question on Zulip](https://rust-lang.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/520710-t-lang.2Ffls/topic/Divergence.20of.20break.20expressions/with/641170911), but I've been looking into divergence of `break` expressions. The Reference says that a break expression diverges, and later has a rule about loops...

TC: Before we continue, are you looking at the [branch](https://github.com/rust-lang/reference/pull/2186) Eric Huss and I have been working on?

Hristian: OK, I'm not looking at the right thing. Anyway, it seemed like break expression diverge by default, but then, through a different semantic, you could arrive at a conclusion that ??.

TC: Let me check out my branch.

Hristian: It's on the page on loop expressions, in the Breaking loop values section.

TC: There's a list of rules for specific divergence behavior. A break expression is diverging and has the type of `!`.

Hristian: This is probably an obsolete rule (?? loop break-value.diverging). The divergence of a break expression seems to depend on the state of its operand, but at the same time the Reference states that a break expression is a diverging expression (i.e. by default). That's a conflict in my opinion.

TC: Within the context of a loop, regardless of whether the break operand diverges, the break is still a diverging expression. The question is whether the loop is a diverging expression.

Hristian: ??

TC: For the break itself, a break is always diverging.

Hristian: Then I need to fix this and the patch that I put in.

TC: Within the ?? scope, the break is like a return and always diverges, but the loop "catches" the divergence there and returns a non-diverging value.

Hristian: Thanks for clarifying this.
